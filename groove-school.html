Sound is generated in your browser, so there is nothing to install. Click an instrument to hear it, or use your computer keyboard on the piano.

Scope, plainly: a dashboard cannot hold every song's full tabs or sheet music. Those are copyrighted and run to thousands of pages. What each sound does

Kick sets the low pulse. Snare and clap mark the backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13 in the grid). Hats keep time and add speed. 808 is a long, tuned bass drum used as a bass note.

Swing pushes every second 16th note late, which gives boom bap its lazy bounce.

Try this: load Boom Bap, then add one extra kick on step 11 and listen to how the feel changes.

Records that became other records

Sampled Songs

Honest scope: there is no single official "top 500 sampled songs" list, and I will not invent rankings. This is a curated set of well-documented examples (about 80, filterable by type), so you can hear where each idea came from. To dig deeper, search any track on WhoSampled.

A short history of sampling

1940s–60sMusique concrète and tape

Composers such as Pierre Schaeffer cut and looped recorded sound on tape. Later, the Mellotron (1963) played tape loops from a keyboard. These were the first "samplers" in spirit.

1973–79The break and the loop

Bronx DJs extended drum breaks with two turntables. The break (the drum-only section of a funk record) became the raw material of hip hop years before any digital sampler existed.

1979–81Replays and records made of records

"Rapper's Delight" (1979) used a studio band replaying Chic's "Good Times". Grandmaster Flash's "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" (1981) was the first widely released record built entirely from other records, cut live on turntables.

1979–85Digital samplers arrive

The Fairlight CMI (1979) and E-mu Emulator (1981) made digital sampling possible but expensive. The Akai S900 (1986) and Ensoniq Mirage (1984) brought the price down for home studios.

1986–89The golden age of the loop

The E-mu SP-1200 (1987) and Akai MPC60 (1988) let producers chop breaks into pads. Marley Marl, the Bomb Squad (Public Enemy), Prince Paul (De La Soul), Eric B. and others layered dozens of samples. Run-DMC and the Beastie Boys sampled rock records.

1991Clearance becomes law

In Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. (the Biz Markie case) a judge ruled that uncleared sampling was theft, opening with "Thou shalt not steal." Labels began requiring clearance of both the master recording and the underlying composition. De La Soul had already been sued by The Turtles in 1989.

1992–96Chopping and the producer as artist

Pete Rock, DJ Premier, RZA, Q-Tip and Large Professor chopped samples into new phrases rather than looping them. Dr. Dre's G-funk mixed samples with live replays. DJ Shadow's "Endtroducing....." (1996) was listed by Guinness as the first album made entirely of samples.

2000sChipmunk soul and Dilla

Kanye West and Just Blaze sped up soul vocals ("chipmunk soul"). J Dilla's "Donuts" (2006) showed loose, off-grid chops as an art form. Bridgeport v. Dimension Films (2005) ruled that any sample of a master, however short, needs a license ("Get a license or do not sample").

2010s–nowSample packs, interpolation and AI

Royalty-free sample libraries and services like Splice made "sampling" legal by default. Interpolation (re-recording a melody rather than lifting the audio) grew because it needs only publishing clearance. In VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone (2016) another court allowed tiny, unrecognizable samples, so the law still differs by region. AI stem separation now lets producers isolate a vocal or drum from any finished record.

Sampling vs interpolation

Sampling lifts the actual audio, so you need permission from the owner of the recording (usually a label) and from the owner of the song (publisher). Interpolation re-plays the melody or lyric with new musicians, so only the song's publisher must agree. "Rapper's Delight" and Dr. Dre's live replays on The Chronic are interpolations.

Techniques

Loop: repeat a bar or two. Chop: slice a phrase into pieces and play them in a new order. Pitch: speed up or slow down to fit a key or tempo (or to create chipmunk soul). Filter: roll off highs or lows so the sample sits under a new bass and drums. Layer: stack a drum break under a melodic loop. Flip: make the source unrecognizable.
